Ji-woo shook. Fury and fear fought for control such that the Viceroy-elect stood frozen, staring at Oni. Her face was a moonscape, inscrutable. The all-black eyes gave him nothing, her gaze on the busy courtyard below. All but the Residence had been unlocked, and few would notice one building of fifty, hardly thirty of a thousand guests missing amid a small, temporary city of strangers. Hideyo’s brow was unfurrowed, despite her accusation.

“I failed *you*?” His mind, awash in adrenaline and cortisol, could do little else but restate her insult with haughty derision. Some incredulous part of him struggled to the fore, taking the reins of his stuttering muscles. “I am the *Viceroy*.” His shoulders squared, neck straightened, eyes bugged, more like an angry customer than an impassioned sovereign. “I do not answer to you, Oni. *You* said you’d deal with them. Now you’ve already lost the Ward. *She* is the problem—I do not *care* if the old woman is dead or not—it is not *she* who is challenging me for the throne!”

Hideyo wheeled on him, grabbing the prince by his narrow, shaking shoulders.

“Understand this, cuckoo. You are Viceroy at *my* pleasure. It is you who owe *me*.”

Her words thundered through his mind, stampeding all other thoughts. Primal terror made real, like a child seeing glowing eyes and hearing a hungry snarl from a darkened closet, threatened his bowel control.

“Every failure has been *yours*. You did not warn us about the old woman. You could not track down your own kin. You failed to secure the palace. You could not even keep the Ward in her room for a handful of hours. I would have taken her. She would have appeared to have run away, her Sifu dead and in disgrace for the busaw she brought with her from the Wilds. We could have even blamed the girl for it. But you, you clown, you utter moron, you merely had to follow my instructions, and even there you fail. What do these photos show but your failure?”

His gaze slid to the corners of his eyes, not daring to turn his face away. On his royal desk, the huge glazed surface designed to dwarf any request to come across it, a vast continent of lawmaking to separate him from all others, lay black-and-white images, minutes old: stills from the armory camera. Every other camera in the Residence had been set to a closed loop, lest any less-than-loyal guard grow agitated at the sight of rampant cannibalism; only the armory’s, tamper-resistant and triggered by motion, still broadcast. In the photos were three women. One he did not recognize, but the other two were, when one looked at all the images together, all too clear. Ward and Sifu, as alive as when they had stormed into the Chamber days ago.

“Remember this, Ji-woo, you mindless buffoon: before me you were a joke. Without your Oni, you are a hopeless failure, doomed to being little more than a punchline. Your sudden rise to power has a cost, and that cost is fealty to *me*. If you are Viceroy, it will be because I permit it.”

Ji-woo stammered.

“Supplicate yourself.”

“I am… Viceroy of Koryo. I don’t—”

“Supplicate”—his knees buckled—“cuckoo.”

Ji-woo had not genuflected since he was a fledgling. The rug fibers, though soft, brushed shame against his forehead and fingertips. There was cowering in this; it was not a humble bow but one wrung from him.

“You believe yourself a wild stallion, full of froth and speed. No, that is Koryo. You believe yourself the rider, then, mounted in the saddle, charging forward. No, that is me. You are reins, you idiot prince. A mere tool, conveying my whims. Say it.”

“I am your tool.” His voice was tremulous.

“Good. Now,” she thought aloud, “listen closely.”

She extended the spine from her wrist. A shimmering drop of venom clung to the tip.

“Did you know there are four types of venom? For much of my life, I did not. Not even after I became an asura.”

Ji-woo, sitting up now, looked on in shock.

“Yes, my stupid little cuckoo prince, I am an asura. That I have had to reveal this to you is why I must explain the consequences of your next failure.” She held the spine close, such that the hollow just under the tip was visible. “Four glands, clustered between my radius and ulna. Neurotoxin, myotoxin, haemotoxin, necrotoxin. I have spent years learning how to shift the mixtures for my purposes. Most interesting is that my neurotoxin is not an anesthetic. You will be unable to move, but the nerves still send signals home. You will still feel pain. I will inject your lips, your eyes, your fingertips with necrotoxin, and each cell will scream in anguish as it dies. There you will be, silent, immobile, wishing every instant for death, but to the outer observer merely asleep in bed, breathing softly, as a babe. Should you fail me, Ji-woo, you will die insane and tormented like few ever have.”

Ji-woo shook, unable to reply.

“Am I understood?”

He nodded, his skull stuttering up and down.

“Excellent. Here is what you shall do.”
